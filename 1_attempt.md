Referee's Note: Why Two Neighbours See Different "Average" Prices

Every morning, two neighbours argue over whether grocery prices have "really" gone up because one quotes the mean and the other quotes the median. [O]Across our 12-week illustrative dataset (January 1 to March 19, 2026), all four tracked items increased in price from the first week to the last: Sweetener D rose by 19.41% (from 152.00 to 181.50 PKR/kg), Oil B rose by 15.28% (from 301.00 to 347.00 PKR/kg), Staple A rose by 12.98% (from 104.00 to 117.50 PKR/kg), and Pulse C rose by 3.69% (from 203.00 to 210.50 PKR/kg). [O]

When comparing the two averages over the entire 12-week window, the numbers are almost identical: Sweetener D has a mean and median of 166.75 PKR/kg, Oil B has a mean of 324.83 PKR/kg and a median of 324.00 PKR/kg, Pulse C has a mean of 205.92 PKR/kg and a median of 206.00 PKR/kg, and Staple A has a mean of 111.17 PKR/kg and a median of 111.75 PKR/kg. **[O]** Both neighbours are looking at valid midpoint summaries of the quarter, yet neither single average captures the steady week-by-week climb shown in the start-to-end percentage changes. [O]

In addition, City 2 prices remained consistently 6.00 PKR/kg higher than City 1 prices for every item on every recorded date.[E] One possible explanation for this constant 6.00 PKR/kg gap is a fixed inter-city transport or retail margin difference, though our price table alone does not record cost breakdowns. [E]Similarly, if a single week contained an extreme price spike or data-entry error, the mean would jump upward while the median would remain anchored near the middle week's price. [O] Therefore, to report prices without misleading readers, the newsroom should publish the median alongside the (first-to-last percentage change) rather than relying on a single average alone. [O]
