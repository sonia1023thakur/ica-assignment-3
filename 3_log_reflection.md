In my initial draft before labeling sentences as observations, explanations, or causal claims, I said more than the data allowed by assuming why the numbers behaved the way they did. Specifically, I was tempted to explain why City 2 was always 6 PKR higher than City 1 by blaming transport or storage costs, and why Sweetener D increased by nearly 20 percent by blaming seasonal demand or inflation. Looking strictly at the cleaned teaching file, the dataset only records the date, item, city, price, unit, and a label stating the values are illustrative. There are no columns for supply, transport, taxes, or consumer demand. Labeling every sentence forced me to remove unsupported causes and stick strictly to what the numbers prove: steady upward price trends across the twelve weeks, a fixed 6 PKR gap between the two cities, and why reporting both the median and the percentage change gives readers a clearer picture than a single average alone.
